About TensorX

AI-Powered ECG Classification System

Project Overview

TensorX is an AI-powered ECG classification system designed to assist healthcare professionals in detecting cardiac abnormalities like Atrial Fibrillation (AFib) and Ventricular Tachycardia (VTCC) in real-time. Built for Orchathon 2k26, this system combines advanced machine learning with medical-grade signal processing.

Our mission is to democratize high-accuracy cardiac monitoring using state-of-the-art machine learning models. By providing an accessible platform for ECG analysis, we aim to assist healthcare professionals in early arrhythmia detection and improve patient outcomes.

ECG Parameters Explained

II

Lead II

ECG Lead II is one of the standard limb leads that records electrical activity between the right arm (negative) and left leg (positive). It provides the best view of the heart's inferior wall and is commonly used for rhythm analysis because it typically shows clear P waves and QRS complexes.

25

Scale: 25mm/s

The paper speed or time scale at which the ECG is recorded. At 25mm/s, each small square (1mm) represents 0.04 seconds (40ms), and each large square (5mm) represents 0.2 seconds (200ms). This is the standard speed for ECG recording in most countries.

Hz

Frequency: 250Hz

The sampling frequency or rate at which the ECG signal is digitized. At 250Hz, the system captures 250 data points per second. This frequency is sufficient to capture all clinically relevant ECG features including P waves, QRS complexes, and T waves without aliasing.

mV

Amplitude: Millivolts

ECG signals are measured in millivolts (mV). Standard calibration is 10mm = 1mV. The typical QRS complex amplitude ranges from 0.5-2.5mV. Our system preserves the original amplitude scaling for accurate clinical interpretation.

AI Classification Classes

Our machine learning model is trained to classify ECG signals into three distinct cardiac rhythm categories:

NSR - Normal Sinus Rhythm

Healthy Cardiac Function

Normal Sinus Rhythm represents a healthy heart rhythm originating from the sinoatrial (SA) node. It is characterized by:

  • Regular rhythm: Consistent R-R intervals with heart rate between 60-100 BPM
  • P wave morphology: Upright P waves in Lead II preceding each QRS complex
  • PR interval: Normal duration (0.12-0.20 seconds)
  • QRS complex: Narrow (<0.12 seconds) and consistent morphology

AFib - Atrial Fibrillation

Irregular Atrial Activity

Atrial Fibrillation is a common cardiac arrhythmia where the atria quiver chaotically instead of contracting effectively. Key features include:

  • Irregularly irregular rhythm: No consistent R-R interval pattern
  • Absent P waves: Replaced by chaotic fibrillatory waves (f-waves)
  • Variable ventricular response: Heart rate typically 80-180 BPM
  • Clinical significance: Increased stroke risk due to blood pooling in atria

VTCC - Ventricular Tachycardia

Life-Threatening Arrhythmia

Ventricular Tachycardia is a potentially life-threatening arrhythmia originating from the ventricles. Critical characteristics:

  • Rapid ventricular rate: Heart rate >100 BPM (typically 120-250 BPM)
  • Wide QRS complexes: Duration >0.12 seconds with abnormal morphology
  • AV dissociation: Atria and ventricles beat independently
  • Emergency status: Can degenerate into ventricular fibrillation and cardiac arrest

Technology Stack

Backend: FastAPI (Python) for high-performance API routing
AI/ML: SVC (Support Vector Classifier) with joblib serialization
Signal Processing: Scipy for Butterworth bandpass filtering & Savitzky-Golay smoothing
LLM Integration: Groq API with Llama 3.3 70B for clinical analysis
Frontend: Jinja2 Templates with Tailwind CSS
Visualization: Plotly.js with WebGL rendering (scattergl)
Database: SQLite for prediction history & audit trails
RAG System: LangChain + FAISS + HuggingFace embeddings
